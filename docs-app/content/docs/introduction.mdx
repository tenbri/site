title: Introduction

---

Tenbri is an open-source, tamper-evident record of what AI agents decided: who acted, under which
rule and policy version, from which inputs, and who approved. It is written in Rust, stores plain
files on a local disk or an object store, and verifies offline against a trust root the verifier
controls.

It works on its own, with any agent framework, from Rust, Python, Node, the browser (WASM), the
`tenbri` CLI or the `tenbrid` REST and gRPC API.

<Callout type="warn" title="Pre-release">

Tenbri is in `0.1.0-beta.3`. Nothing is ready for production yet, and the format is provisional
until its external review ends. See the
[roadmap](https://github.com/tenbri/tenbri#status).

</Callout>

## The problem

Companies now give agents real authority: refunds, payments, account changes, production deploys,
security responses. Afterwards they cannot prove what an agent did, what it saw, which rule allowed
it, or who signed off.

- **A customer disputes a refusal.** Support can show the reply, but not which policy version,
  contract clause and permission produced it.
- **An incident happens.** Engineers rebuild the story from logs in five services, each editable by
  the people being investigated.
- **A policy changes.** Nobody can list the decisions made under the old wording.
- **An auditor asks for evidence.** Screenshots and CSV exports prove nothing about integrity.
- **A prompt injection makes an agent act.** There is no record of what the agent read.

## Non-goals

Multi-writer consensus, a blockchain or token, a general-purpose database, storing raw telemetry
or full prompts by default, judging whether a decision was correct, telemetry of any kind, and a
hosted service.
