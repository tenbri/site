A stream cannot vouch for itself. Tenbri makes every change detectable by a verifier that holds a
**trust root**: the fingerprint of the stream's genesis key, and any checkpoint it saw before.

## Canonical bytes

Every stored entry and every HEAD is one JSON object in its canonical form, as defined by
[RFC 8785](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8785) (JSON Canonicalization Scheme). In practice:
no whitespace outside strings, members sorted by name, one fixed spelling for each string and
integer.

A stored line must equal the canonical form of its own parse, byte for byte. A verifier parses the
line, serialises it again and compares. Any difference fails with `not_canonical`, even an extra
space that changes nothing in the parsed value.

Format v1 adds three limits:

- **No duplicate keys**, at any depth (`duplicate_key`).
- **Integers only**: `1.0`, `1e2` and `-0` fail with `not_integer`; integers outside
  ±(2^53 - 1) fail with `integer_range`.
- **Valid UTF-8**: invalid bytes, a lone surrogate or a byte order mark fail with `parse`.

`tenbri append` canonicalises the JSON you give it. Duplicate keys and non-integers are rejected
there too, never repaired.

## Hash chain

Each entry's **entry hash** is the SHA-256 of a zero byte followed by the stored line (signature
included, line feed excluded):

```text
entry hash = SHA-256(0x00 || stored line)
```

This is the [RFC 6962](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6962) leaf hash, so the same value is
the leaf of the Merkle tree below. It is written `sha256:` and 64 hex digits.

Every entry after the first carries `prev`, the entry hash of the entry before it. Editing,
inserting, deleting or reordering an entry breaks the chain at that point: a wrong `prev` fails
with `bad_prev`, a `seq` that is not the previous plus one fails with `bad_seq`.

## Signatures

Every entry is signed with Ed25519. The signed message is a domain prefix, a zero byte and the
canonical entry without its `sig` member:

```text
message = "tenbri/entry/v1" || 0x00 || canonical(entry without "sig")
```

The signature covers every other member, `prev` and `signer` included. Verifiers use strict
verification (RFC 8032), and a failure is `bad_signature`.

Which key may sign what:

- `key` entries are signed by the current **stream key**.
- Every other entry is signed by a **session key** the stream key certified.

A signer no earlier entry certified fails with `unknown_signer`; a key signing outside its role
fails with `signer_not_allowed`. Expiry and revocation are in [Keys](/docs/concepts/keys/).

**Attestations** are extra signatures by the principal or an approver, over the entry's draft
(without `seq`, `prev`, `signer`, `sig` and `attestations`) and under their own prefix,
`tenbri/attestation/v1`. The writer's signature covers them. Format v1 checks that each
attestation verifies; whom the attesting key belongs to is for the verifier to decide.

## HEAD

HEAD is a small signed object at the root of the stream:

```json
{"batch":4,"counter":4,"hash":"sha256:c86c6372…","seq":4,"sig":"ed25519:76a6cc6a…","signer":"sess:2026-10-20:db131c41f8ad","stream":"tnb1:5db31441baa2d080326d9f6c83c95319","v":1}
```

It names the latest `seq`, its entry `hash`, the first `seq` of the `batch` holding it, and a
`counter` that increases at every HEAD write. It is signed by the stream key or an unrevoked
session key, under the prefix `tenbri/head/v1`, so an entry signature can never pass as a HEAD
signature. A writer advances HEAD by compare-and-swap.

A HEAD that does not match the stream, is unsigned, or has a lower counter than one the reader
already saw fails with `bad_head`. A batch HEAD needs but the store lacks fails with
`missing_batch`.

## Checkpoints

A **checkpoint** is a signed statement of a stream's size and the Merkle root of its first `size`
entries. The tree is the RFC 6962 Merkle hash tree (as RFC 9162 defines it) over the entry hashes:
entry `seq` is leaf `seq - 1`.

Checkpoints are [C2SP signed notes](https://c2sp.org/signed-note) in the
[C2SP checkpoint format](https://c2sp.org/tlog-checkpoint), so existing transparency-log tools can
read them. The note text is three lines: the origin (the stream id), the size, and the root in
base64:

```text
tnb1:5db31441baa2d080326d9f6c83c95319
4
Y8klGLPJWRoPh0XjwIDZtH95fpxRKlyS7MqHr2abP1g=
```

An empty line and one or more signature lines follow. The stream's signature line starts with the
character U+2014, a space, the origin as key name, a space, then the base64 of a 4-byte key ID and
the Ed25519 signature. A session key signs it; the stream key never signs checkpoints, so it can
stay offline. Witnesses and auditors can add their own cosignature lines to the same note.

A writer writes a checkpoint after advancing HEAD once 1,000 entries were added since the last
one, or once at least one entry was added and 60 seconds passed, whichever comes first. It is
stored as `checkpoints/<size, 12 digits>.note` and never rewritten. A writer also keeps the
latest checkpoint it wrote next to the keys (`checkpoint.note` in the stream's key directory) and
refuses to open a stream that fails against it: an older HEAD put back by someone with access to
the store fails with `truncated`.

<Aside type="note">
`tenbri checkpoint <stream>`, which seals a checkpoint on demand, is on the `main` branch and not
in `0.1.0-alpha.2`. The 60 seconds count from when the writer opened the stream or last wrote a
checkpoint, so a one-shot `tenbri append` does not write one. A long-running writer (`tenbrid`,
the Rust `Writer`) does.
</Aside>

A checkpoint only protects what it covers once someone outside the operator holds it: an auditor,
a witness, or a verifier that saw it before.

<Aside type="caution" title="Planned">
Publishing checkpoints to auditors, witnesses, RFC 3161 timestamps and receipts (self-contained
proofs of single entries) are not in a release yet.
</Aside>

## What verification checks

`tenbri verify <stream> --key <fingerprint>` reads the stream from HEAD and checks each entry, in
order, stopping at the first failure:

1. canonical bytes;
2. top-level members, field rules and kind rules;
3. `seq`;
4. for entry 1, the genesis rules and the pinned fingerprint (`untrusted_root` on a mismatch);
   for the others, `stream`, then `prev`;
5. `id` uniqueness;
6. the signer: certified, allowed for this kind, not revoked, not expired;
7. the signature, then each attestation;
8. `hlc`;
9. intents, reconciles and approvals, then key events.

Then it checks HEAD. If the stream has checkpoints, it also checks the stream against its own
latest one. A revocation that reaches back is reported at the earliest entry it invalidates.

`--key` takes the fingerprint (`sha256:…`) or the public key (`ed25519:…`) of the genesis key.

### Exit codes

| Code | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `0` | Verified against the pinned key (and the checkpoint, if given) |
| `1` | The stream does not verify, or does not match the `--checkpoint` given. The first broken sequence number and the error code are printed |
| `2` | Any other error: a missing stream, a bad `--key`, a malformed checkpoint file |
| `3` | No `--key`: the stream agrees with its own genesis key, which proves nothing about who wrote it |

A failure looks like this:

```text
broken at seq 3: bad_signature: the signature does not verify
2 entries verified before it, stream tnb1:5db31441baa2d080326d9f6c83c95319
```

The full list of codes is in the [error reference](/docs/reference/errors/).

## Truncation and forks

Without checkpoints, the chain and signatures detect any edit, reordering, insertion or deletion
before HEAD, forged entries, and entries signed by a wrong, expired, revoked or uncertified key.

They do not detect two things a key holder can do: cut the tail and re-sign HEAD, or rewrite the
whole stream with the stream key.

A checkpoint you hold closes that gap for the entries it covers:

```sh
tenbri verify audit/support --key sha256:5db31441… --checkpoint 000000000004.note
```

```text
matches checkpoint tnb1:5db31441baa2d080326d9f6c83c95319 at size 4
matches its latest checkpoint, at size 4
verified 4 entries, stream tnb1:5db31441baa2d080326d9f6c83c95319, pinned to sha256:5db31441…
```

- **`truncated`**: the stream has fewer entries than the checkpoint covers. Reported at the first
  missing `seq`.
- **`fork`**: a Merkle consistency proof from the checkpoint to the stream's current tree fails,
  so entries 1 to `size` are not the ones the checkpoint signed. Reported at `size`; the root alone
  cannot say where the rewrite starts.
- `bad_checkpoint`, `unknown_signer`, `signer_not_allowed` or `key_revoked`: the checkpoint is
  malformed or not validly signed by a session key of this stream.

Two holders shown different histories also catch it: their checkpoints are not consistent.
Anything after a checkpoint's size, including truncation back to exactly that size, is not
covered by it; a later checkpoint held outside the operator moves the line.

A checkpoint adds to the pinned key; it does not replace it. `verify --checkpoint` without `--key`
still exits 3.
